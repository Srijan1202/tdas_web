TDAS hardware pilot · Raspberry Pi 4 Model B · preregistered · 18 Oct 2026 PREREGISTERED VERDICT: LIKELY KILL

Reordering jobs moved the heat.
It did not move the latency.

We ran the same 600 jobs in four different orders on a passively cooled Raspberry Pi 4, to see whether thermal-aware ordering lowers tail latency. Ordering changed how long the chip spent above its throttling point. Tail latency, mean latency and throughput did not change in any statistically supported way.

Where the experiment lives on the temperature scale (°C)
The setup

Same work, different order

Every sequence holds 300 HOT jobs and 300 COOL jobs. HOT keeps all four cores busy (CPU duty 0.99). COOL does a third of the arithmetic and sleeps the rest of the time (duty 0.38). When the chip is cold both take the same time: .

Each sequence starts after a cooldown, then a warm-up to 77 °C, just below the knee. There are four orderings, each run 10 times in an interleaved, counterbalanced schedule: 40 sequences and 24,000 jobs in about 3 hours.

The hypotheses, the six gates and the verdict rule were fixed in writing before the data. Every later change was recorded as a dated amendment.

The preregistered gates

Four pass, two fail, one cannot be tested

Finding 1 · G4

Ordering changes the thermal trajectory

Hot-first climbs toward the knee during its HOT half. Cool-first first cools, then climbs. Alternating and random settle near 71–72 °C and never cross it.

Finding 2 · G5 and G5b

The tail stays put

Median P99 per sequence ranges from 201.3 to 202.1 ms across the four orderings. Uncorrected, one pair's interval excludes zero. After the preregistered Holm correction over six pairs, no pair does.

Mean latency (197.1–197.2 ms) and throughput (5.013–5.015 jobs/s) show no supported difference either.

A non-significant result is not proof that no effect exists. With 10 sequences per ordering, differences smaller than about 1–5 ms could have been missed.

Why the heat did not reach the tail · exploratory, cannot change the verdict

The slowdown is a step, and this run rarely reached it

Experiment 2 shows that latency is flat below the knee and steps up only on the throttling plateau. That plateau needs minutes of continuous load. A hot-first sequence runs at most 60 s of HOT work.

    The board's thermal state shifted mid-session

    In the first ~70 minutes, cooldowns took 152–262 s (median per repetition), and 5 of 6 hot-first and cool-first sequences spent 32–37 s above the knee. Their P99 was 205.5–210.8 ms. After that, cooldowns took 57–75 s and P99 sat at 200.1–202.5 ms in every ordering.

    The cause is unknown: room temperature and airflow were not measured. Every repetition held all four orderings, so the shift adds noise rather than bias. It points to a hypothesis for a future, separately preregistered study: when a sequence spends more than ~30 s above the knee, ordering might move P99 by a few milliseconds.

    What this does and does not show

    Supported

    • A throttle knee at 80.8 °C, read from the firmware ARM clock. The Linux frequency file showed 1800 MHz throughout and missed it.
    • Service time rises with temperature, as a step at the knee (+6–8 % on the plateau).
    • Job order changes the time spent above the knee (13.3 s against 0 s).
    • At this operating point, job order did not produce a statistically supported change in P99, P95, mean latency or throughput.

    Not shown, or limited

    • That ordering never affects latency. Small effects, or effects at other thermal states, remain possible.
    • Results beyond one device, one synthetic workload, one start temperature (77 °C) and one sequence length (600 jobs).
    • The cooling hardware on the test day, and the room temperature, were not recorded.
    • With 600 jobs, P99 is the 7th-slowest job, so it reacts only when several jobs are slowed.

    Method. Raspberry Pi 4 Model B Rev 1.4, Debian 13, kernel 6.18.50, governor performance. Temperature was sampled from sysfs at 5 Hz, and throttle flags were read between jobs. A watchdog that starts no processes enforced an 85 °C stop. Statistics: the unit of analysis is the sequence; percentile bootstrap with 10,000 resamples and seed 0; two-sided bootstrap p-values; Holm correction; Cliff's δ.
